### Objective

Setting a nightly shutdown across a wall is the most laborious thing in Polyptic. Panel power is per-screen today: one `PUT /api/v1/screens/:screenId/panel-hours` per screen, one control per screen row, and no bulk path — `bulkAction` covers reboot, arm, disarm, ident and approve, and nothing else. A wall where every screen keeps the same hours is the normal case and the worst-served one.

The scenes view already owns the machinery panel hours never got: named dayparts, weekday and date-range recurrence, integer priority, one deployment timezone, and a resolver in `@polyptic/protocol` shared by the server's ticker and the console's week strip. Panel power moves onto that engine, as a property of the schedule window.

**Done means:** an operator opens Scenes, adds one window that says *these days, this daypart, panels off*, and every screen on that mural sleeps and wakes on it — with a manual wake at 19:05 still holding until the next boundary, and a box that reboots at 3am still coming back dark.

### The load-bearing choice: power hangs on the window, not the scene

A scene is a snapshot of what plays. If a scene carried a power state, every scene would acquire a hidden power dimension, and the rule `panel-power.ts` is built around — *a screen that should be showing content must never blank* — would stop being structural and become something each scene has to not get wrong. Hung on the window, "off" is only ever what an operator explicitly scheduled.

<Callout id="decision-window" tone="decision">

`panels` lives on `Schedule`, defaulting to `"on"`, so every stored schedule keeps today's behaviour. A window is the only thing that can darken a wall.

</Callout>

### Resolution becomes per-mural, and reads the same candidate list twice

`resolveAt` today runs one total order over every schedule in the deployment and returns exactly one winning scene; `applyScene` then touches only that scene's mural. Schedules on different murals genuinely compete, so a second mural's window can lose forever and never take the wall. For content that is a latent wart. For power it is fatal: a `panels` flag on a schedule could only ever speak for whichever single window won globally, so "the fleet sleeps at 19:00" would not be expressible on a deployment with more than one mural.

Resolution therefore runs per mural. The candidate list for a mural is sorted by the existing total order — priority, then most-recently-started, then shorter, then older — and read twice:

- **content** is the best candidate carrying a `sceneId`, falling through to the default scene and then to nothing, exactly as today;
- **power** is the `panels` value of the *overall* winner, power-only windows included.

A power-only window is one with no `sceneId`: *don't change what plays, only set the panels*. It is what makes a nightly shutdown expressible without inventing a scene to bind it to, and it is why an off window never disturbs the content underneath — the wall wakes onto the scene that was already resolved for it.

When **no enabled window governs a mural at all**, power resolves to `null`: ungoverned, leave the wall exactly as it is. That preserves POL-101's rule that a disabled window means "the schedule does not govern this screen", which is not the same as "keep it awake". Once a mural has any window, the gaps between windows resolve to `"on"` — and that is what wakes the wall at 07:00 when the nightly off window ends.

<Callout id="risk-wake" tone="risk">

The gap-resolves-to-on rule is the single most behaviour-changing line in the plan. Get it wrong in the other direction and a wall that slept at 19:00 never wakes. It needs a resolver test that walks a full 24h and asserts the `on` edge at the exact end of the off window.

</Callout>

DST stays a non-event for the same reason it already is: containment is pure wall-clock in the deployment's one IANA zone, and the ticker fires only on a change of verdict, so a repeated fall-back hour re-resolves identically and a window spanning the spring-forward gap stays covered.

The default scene stays a single deployment-wide setting, scoped to the mural it belongs to: it fills the gaps on that mural and no other. A second mural is simply ungoverned until it gets a window of its own, and the Settings copy says so rather than implying a fleet-wide floor it does not provide. A default scene per mural is the eventual end state, but it is another settings surface and another migration in a change that already carries four.

Three columns are new. `panels` is `'on' | 'off'`, defaulting to `'on'`. `scene_id` becomes nullable — a null is a power-only window. `mural_id` is denormalised: it is copied from the scene at create time for scene-bearing windows and never edited afterwards (a scene cannot move mural), and it is the operator's explicit target for a power-only window, where a null means every mural. `murals.active_scene_id` replaces the single global `meta.active_scene_id`, because per-mural resolution means each mural is on its own scene. All four are additive `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS` migrations plus a one-row backfill of the old `meta.active_scene_id` into whichever mural owned that scene.

<Callout id="decision-muralid" tone="decision">

`mural_id` is stored on the schedule rather than looked up through the scene, so the resolver in `@polyptic/protocol` stays a pure function of `ScheduleSet` and the console never needs a scene→mural join to paint the strip.

</Callout>

<Callout id="decision-active-scene" tone="decision">

`DesiredState.activeSceneId` is replaced outright rather than kept alongside the per-mural map. Two fields that can disagree — one of them meaning "whichever mural most recently changed" — is the kind of thing that reads fine now and misleads later. It is a breaking protocol change across five call sites, which is cheap while nothing outside the console consumes it.

</Callout>

### Per-screen panel hours are removed

Nothing in the fleet currently has panel hours set, so there is no migration to get honest — the feature is deleted rather than converted. `PanelHours`, `PUT /api/v1/screens/:screenId/panel-hours`, the `panel_power.hours` blob and the per-screen control in `ScreenRow.vue` all go. `PanelPowerConfig.timezone` collapses into `SchedulerSettings.timezone`, leaving the deployment with one timezone instead of two that could disagree.

Manual wake and sleep is untouched: `POST /api/v1/screens/:screenId/display-power`, the agent's `agent/power-ack`, the sleeping chip, and `powerAckLine`'s honesty about DPMS-only boxes leaving panels lit-but-black all stay exactly as they are.
